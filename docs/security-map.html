How every part of DynaNotch is kept safe

DynaNotch lives in your Mac's notch and is trusted with a lot: your clipboard, your notes, your camera, and permission to control other apps. This map shows every way something from outside can reach it, the guard that stands in the way, and what that guard lets through or stops.

Links in websites and emails

Any web page or email can contain a special dynanotch:// link that talks to the app.

Guard

Only harmless buttons work from outside

Like a shop window: outsiders can ring the bell, but they can't walk into the back room. Links may only do things that can't hurt you, and only a few at a time.

Allowed: show a short message, open the panel, open a harmless tab, start a timer.
Stopped: changing settings, clicking buttons for you, approving Claude Code, turning on the camera, showing your clipboard or notes, crashing the app, flooding you with pop-ups (more than 8 in 10 seconds).
Tested: 10 attack links in every test run, all refused.

Your own scripts and tests

You (or the test suite) sometimes need full control, for example to check every screen automatically.

Guard

A private door with your name on it

Full control goes through a private door inside your user folder. The Mac itself checks who is knocking: only programs running as you get in. There is no password that could leak.

Allowed: your own scripts and the automatic tests.
Stopped: other user accounts on the Mac, websites, anything that isn't you.

Other programs

A sneaky program could try to slip its own code inside DynaNotch to borrow DynaNotch's permissions (camera, microphone, controlling your Mac).

Guard

A sealed app ("hardened runtime")

DynaNotch is sealed by macOS: no outside code can be loaded into it, and it only holds the five permissions its features need.

Allowed: DynaNotch's own code and Apple's system parts.
Stopped: injected code, unsigned add-ons, debuggers attaching to it.
Tested: a planted add-on got into an unsealed copy and was blocked by the real app.
Comes from

App updates

DynaNotch can update itself from the internet. A fake update would be the easiest way to take over.

Guard

Four checks, like a passport control

1. It comes from DynaNotch's own release page, over a secure connection.
2. Its fingerprint matches the one GitHub published.
3. It carries the owner's personal seal. The seal's key lives only in the owner's Keychain.
4. The app inside is really DynaNotch.

Allowed: genuine releases that pass all four checks.
Stopped: downloads from anywhere else, damaged or changed files, and even a release uploaded by someone who broke into the GitHub account (they don't have the seal).
Tested: changing one bit of an update makes the seal fail.
Comes from

Files you drop on the notch

Drop Alchemy turns pictures and PDFs into cutouts, text or smaller copies. A booby-trapped file could attack the program that opens it.

Guard

Opened in a sealed room

Files are opened by a separate helper locked in a sandbox: no internet, no reading your files, no saving anywhere except its own scratch space, no access to your permissions.

Allowed: the finished picture, text or PDF, which DynaNotch then puts next to your file.
Stopped: a hijacked helper reading your files or clipboard, writing to your Desktop, or going online.
Tested from inside the sealed room: all four escape attempts failed.
Comes from

Claude Code asking for permission

An AI coding assistant asks "may I run this command?" in the notch. A trick could hide the dangerous part.

Guard

What you see is what runs

Invisible and back-to-front characters are removed, long gaps are squeezed out, and the end of a long command is always shown. The buttons ignore clicks for the first second, so a request that pops up under your mouse can't be approved by accident.

Allowed: your deliberate Allow, Deny or Terminal click.
Stopped: hidden commands, disguised text, accidental approvals, websites answering for you.
Comes from

Things you copy

DynaNotch keeps a clipboard history. People copy passwords, keys and card numbers all the time.

Guard

A secret spotter

Every copy is checked. Passwords, private keys, access tokens, card numbers and bank numbers are never written down. One-time codes disappear after 2 minutes. Everything else clears after a day, or when you lock the Mac if you choose.

Allowed: ordinary text, links, images and files.
Stopped: secrets ending up in your history; password managers are ignored completely.
Tested with fake keys and card numbers: none were saved.
Comes from

Someone with a copy of your files

A backup disk, a cloud folder or a stolen hard drive could contain DynaNotch's saved data.

Guard

Locked with a key inside your Mac's chip

Clipboard history, notes, to-dos and other history are encrypted. The key lives in the Mac's security chip (the Secure Enclave) and can never be copied out. The folder is also closed to other users.

Allowed: DynaNotch on this Mac, with no password prompts. Notes can be exported to move them.
Stopped: reading the data on any other computer or from any backup.
Checked: every data file on the owner's Mac is encrypted; all 50 clipboard items still opened.

Screen sharing and system logs

Video calls, screen recordings and the Mac's diagnostic logs can show what's on screen or in the app.

Guard

Invisible to cameras, quiet in logs

The notch panel is hidden from screen shares and recordings (you still see it). Names, titles and file paths are blanked out in the Mac's logs and bug reports.

Allowed: you seeing everything on your own screen.
Stopped: people on a call seeing your clipboard or notes; personal details in shared logs.

Honest limits

What no app can stop

  • A harmful program you already installed and run as yourself can do what you can do.
  • Someone who knows your Mac password and has your Mac in hand.
  • Bugs inside macOS itself.
  • On a new Mac, old encrypted history can't be read. That is by design, so export your notes first.
